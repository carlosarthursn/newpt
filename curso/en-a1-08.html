Leitura: Dragonfly, a helicopter for Titan (notícia de novembro de 2026)

Titan is Saturn's largest moon, and its nights are almost eight Earth days long. NASA's next mission there is Dragonfly, a helicopter.

Dragonfly now has its seven science cameras. A company, Malin Space Science Systems, makes them, and the Dragonfly team tests them at APL, a laboratory of Johns Hopkins University.

Each camera has a job. The two cameras on the front work together and give a 3D view, and the cameras on the sides take panoramic photos. The cameras under the helicopter check the ground: is it safe for a landing? A microscopic camera looks at the drill, and this small camera sees very small grains of sand.

For those long nights, Dragonfly has a panel of LED lights, from infrared to ultraviolet. With this light, the colors of the ground give clues about its chemistry, and some organic molecules glow under ultraviolet light.

Dragonfly's launch is in July 2028, and its arrival at Titan is at the end of 2034. That's a long trip!
