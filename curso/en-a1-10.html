Gramática: advérbios de frequência, as horas e os dias da semana

Na Lição 7 você viu expressões como every day e at night. Agora vêm os advérbios de frequência, que dizem com que frequência algo acontece, e o kit para marcar horário: as horas, os dias da semana e as preposições at, on e in.

1. Advérbios de frequência: do 100% ao 0%

AdvérbioFrequênciaExemplo
always (sempre)100%I always have coffee in the morning.
usually (geralmente)cerca de 90%She usually goes to bed at eleven.
often (com frequência)cerca de 70%We often play games online.
sometimes (às vezes)cerca de 50%I sometimes listen to jazz.
rarely, hardly ever (raramente, quase nunca)cerca de 10%He rarely watches TV.
never (nunca)0%Astronauts on the ISS never take a shower.

hardly ever (HÁRD-li É-vâr) é mais comum na fala do que rarely. Para perguntar, use How often...? (Com que frequência...?). Número exato de vezes: once, twice, three times..., com o a no papel de "por", no fim da frase: How often do you play games? — Twice a week.

2. A posição: a regra de ouro. Com o to be, o advérbio vem depois do verbo. Com os outros verbos, vem antes do verbo principal. Em português dá para dizer "sempre acordo cedo" ou "acordo sempre cedo"; em inglês, o lugar é um só.

Com to be: depois do verboCom os outros verbos: antes do verbo
I'm always tired on Mondays.I always wake up early.
She's never late.She never eats meat.
The astronauts are often busy.The astronauts often do experiments.
The lab is usually closed on Sundays.The lab usually opens at eight.

Na negativa e na pergunta, o advérbio continua colado antes do verbo principal (depois do don't / doesn't e do sujeito). E o -s da 3ª pessoa continua no verbo:

I don't usually study at night. (Não costumo estudar à noite.) · Do you often play online? · Is she always late? · She always wakes up early. (nunca "she always wake up")

Sometimes é o mais solto: pode ir também no começo ou no fim (Sometimes I play until two in the morning. · until = até), e usually também pode abrir a frase. Always e never, não: ✘ "Always I wake up early." → I always wake up early.

3. As horas. "Que horas são?" é What time is it?, no singular e com time, e a resposta começa com It's (o it "vazio" da Lição 1). Há o jeito digital, que é só ler os números (o mais fácil, e todo mundo entende), e o tradicional, com past (depois de) e to (para):

RelógioJeito digital (o mais fácil)Jeito tradicional
7:00It's seven. / It's seven o'clock.It's seven o'clock.
7:05It's seven oh five.It's five past seven.
7:15It's seven fifteen.It's a quarter past seven.
7:30It's seven thirty.It's half past seven.
7:45It's seven forty-five.It's a quarter to eight.

O zero dos minutos se lê oh (ôu), como a letra O. No jeito tradicional, até os 30 minutos se usa past; depois, to (twenty to eight = vinte para as oito). A quarter leva a; half, não. E half past seven é 7:30 (se você também estuda alemão, cuidado: lá, halb sieben é 6:30!).

a.m. e p.m. No inglês do dia a dia, o relógio vai só até 12: a.m. (êi-ÉM, do latim ante meridiem, "antes do meio-dia") vai da meia-noite ao meio-dia, e p.m. (pí-ÉM, post meridiem), do meio-dia à meia-noite: 7h = 7 a.m.; 15h = 3 p.m.; 21h30 = 9:30 p.m. (com dois-pontos, nunca "9h30"). Para 12h e 0h, prefira noon e midnight, porque 12 a.m. e 12 p.m. confundem até nativo. Na fala, também vale in the morning / in the afternoon / in the evening / at night (three in the afternoon = 15h), mas não junte as duas coisas: ✘ "9 p.m. at night".

A hora de alguma coisa: What time + do / does + sujeito + verbo?, com resposta em at:

What time do you wake up? — At seven. · What time does the class start? — At 2 p.m. · What time is it? — It's ten thirty. (✘ "At ten thirty.")

4. Os dias da semana vão sempre com maiúscula, como os meses: Monday (MÂN-dêi, segunda-feira), Tuesday (TÚZ-dêi, terça), Wednesday (UÉNZ-dêi, quarta; o primeiro d e o segundo e não soam), Thursday (THÂRZ-dêi, quinta), Friday (FRÁI-dêi, sexta), Saturday (SÉ-dâr-dêi, sábado) e Sunday (SÂN-dêi, domingo).

Curiosidade de astrônomo: os nomes vêm dos sete "astros errantes" dos antigos: Sunday é o dia do Sol, Monday, o da Lua (Moon), e Saturday, o de Saturno. Nos outros, os povos germânicos puseram deuses nórdicos no lugar dos romanos: Tiw (Týr) no de Marte, Woden (Odin) no de Mercúrio, Thor no de Júpiter e Frigg no de Vênus (compare: martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, em espanhol).

5. at, on e in com tempo, do mais preciso para o mais amplo:

PreposiçãoComExemplos
athoras e momentos exatosat six, at 9:30, at noon, at midnight, at night
ondias e datason Monday, on Fridays, on November 25, on my birthday, on weekends
inpartes do dia, meses, anos, estaçõesin the morning, in the evening, in November, in 2027, in the summer

Dia + parte do dia fica com on (on Monday morning), e o dia no plural vira hábito (on Mondays = toda segunda). At night é a exceção das partes do dia. Sem preposição: every day, today (hoje), tomorrow (amanhã), next week (semana que vem). Datas se leem com ordinal (November 25 = November twenty-fifth). Do menor para o maior: The rocket lifts off at 8:18 a.m. on Wednesday, November 25.

Cinco armadilhas de brasileiro

1. Advérbio no lugar errado. "I go always to the gym" → I always go to the gym. Com to be, é o contrário: "He always is late" → He is always late.

2. Negativa dupla. Never já é negativo: "I don't never study on Sundays" → I never study on Sundays.

3. "What hours are?" É "Que horas são?" palavra por palavra → What time is it? Na resposta, nada de hours: "It's nine hours" → It's nine (o'clock). Hour é a duração: two hours of exercise.

4. Preposição trocada. "in Monday", "at the morning", "on November", "on every day" → on Monday, in the morning, in November, every day.

5. Agenda à brasileira. "at 21h", "on monday", "one time for week" → at 9 p.m., on Monday (com maiúscula), once a week.

Leitura: An astronaut's schedule (rotina real e notícia de novembro de 2026)

What time is it on the International Space Station (ISS)? The ISS goes around the Earth about every 90 minutes, so the astronauts see about 16 sunrises and 16 sunsets every day. For their schedule, they use GMT (Greenwich Mean Time), the same time as London in winter.

A normal day on the ISS usually starts at six o'clock in the morning. The astronauts wake up, wash and have breakfast. They never take a shower, because there isn't a shower on the station: they use wet towels and a special shampoo. After breakfast, they usually have a short meeting with the control centers on Earth, and then they start work.

What do they do all day? They often do experiments in biology, physics and chemistry. They sometimes fix machines, and they rarely go outside: a spacewalk is a big event. And every day, they exercise for about two hours, on a bike, a treadmill and a weight machine. Why? Because in space, bones and muscles get weak.

They have lunch in the middle of the day. The food usually comes in packets, and the astronauts often eat tortillas, not bread: bread makes crumbs, and crumbs fly everywhere in microgravity! In the evening, they often have dinner together, and they usually go to bed at about half past nine.

On weekends, the schedule is lighter. On Saturdays, the astronauts usually clean the station. On Sundays, they often rest, read, call their families or look at the Earth from the Cupola, a module with seven windows.

And in China? China has a space station too: Tiangong. In November 2026, it has a special visitor. Here is the schedule of the Shenzhou-24 mission:

Wednesday, November 25, 8:18 a.m. (Beijing time): a Long March 2F rocket lifts off from Jiuquan, in China, with three astronauts. In Brasília, it's 9:18 p.m. on Tuesday, November 24.

About six hours later: the astronauts are inside the station. The crew: commander Tang Hongbo, flight engineer Wu Yi and Zeeshan Ali, a 40-year-old pilot from Pakistan. Ali is the first foreign astronaut on Tiangong. His training? Seven months in Beijing, with Chinese classes and even ping-pong.

On the station: Ali stays for 5 to 10 days. One of his experiments tests self-healing carbon fiber composites, materials that close small cracks in the structure of spacecraft by themselves. Then he goes back to Earth with the rest of the Shenzhou-23 crew. And Zhu Yangzhu, the commander of Shenzhou-23? He stays on Tiangong for about a year: a new Chinese record for time in orbit.

And you? What time is it now? What day is it today?
