Gramática: for, since e present perfect continuous

Em português, para algo que começou no passado e continua, usamos o presente: "Estudo inglês há dois anos", "Moro aqui desde 2020". Em inglês, o presente sozinho não alcança o passado: é preciso o present perfect, a ponte entre o passado e o agora.

The probe has been traveling for eight years. → A sonda está viajando há oito anos. (✘ The probe is traveling for eight years.)

Present perfect continuous = have / has been + verbo com -ing: It has (It's) been sending only a carrier signal. · It hasn't been sending any data. · How long has it been traveling? Serve para:

Atividade que começou no passado e continua: I've been studying English for two years. → Estudo inglês há dois anos.

Atividade recente que deixou marcas: Your eyes are red. Have you been reading all night? → Seus olhos estão vermelhos. Você passou a noite lendo?

Simple ou continuous? O continuous destaca a atividade; o simple, o resultado ou a quantidade: I've been writing my report all morning (ainda escrevendo) x I've written three pages (quanto já foi feito). Verbos de estado (be, know, like, believe, understand, have no sentido de "possuir") ficam só no simple: I've known him since school (✘ I've been knowing him).

O "há" do português tem duas traduções. Fato terminado = past simple + ago: "A sonda chegou há cinco dias" = The probe arrived five days ago. Situação que continua = present perfect + for: "A sonda está em órbita há cinco dias" = The probe has been in orbit for five days.

How long, não how much time: "Há quanto tempo você estuda engenharia?" = How long have you been studying engineering? (✘ How much time do you study engineering?)

Armadilha: ✘ I study here since 2022. ✔ I've been studying here since 2022. E since com duração não existe: ✘ since two years ✔ for two years; ✘ for 2020 ✔ since 2020. Teste: se cabe em "desde quando?", é since; em "por quanto tempo?", é for.

Since + oração (no past simple): I've been reading a lot since I started college. E "faz tempo que não...": It's been five years since I last went to a concert = I haven't been to a concert for five years.

Leitura: Far from home (notícias de novembro de 2026)

One light-day from Earth. On November 18, Voyager 1 became the first human-made object to reach a distance of one light-day from Earth: about 25.9 billion kilometers. This means that a command from Earth now takes 24 hours to get there, and the answer needs another 24 hours to come back. But there's a plot twist: since November 14, the probe has been sending only its carrier signal, with no science or engineering data. NASA suspects the onboard computer that collects and formats the data, but the team says it is confident that the data will come back. Ironically, this "empty" signal, together with trajectory calculations, helped confirm the milestone.

Finally at Mercury. The European-Japanese probe BepiColombo entered orbit around Mercury on November 21, after a journey of about eight years and nine flybys: one of Earth, two of Venus and six of Mercury itself. It is only the second spacecraft to orbit Mercury, after NASA's MESSENGER (2011–2015). The arrival wasn't perfect: a software problem cut the engine burn short, so the probe is now in a 75-hour orbit instead of the planned 58-hour one. Still, ESA says it is safe and in a stable orbit. Its two orbiters, Europe's MPO and Japan's Mio, have been flying together for about eight years, and they will separate on December 9. Has the real science started yet? Not yet: it only begins in April 2027.

Dica de pronúncia

No inglês americano, been soa curtinho e sem força, "bin": I've been studying = "áiv bin STÂ-di-in(g)". Since é "sins", sem vogal no fim (nada de "SÍN-ci"). E for forte soa igual a four (fór), mas no meio da frase enfraquece: for two years = "fâr tú IÍRZ".
